Sapphire Bridge · Causal Decidability

Method applied
to TaxiGPT.

We ruled out four simple stories about why the virtual taxi driver loses their bearings after a memory edit; separate K/V tests on 512 fresh routes excluded all four response rules left open by the joint intervention.

The model and released checkpoint come from Vafa et al. (2024) (source repository).

The method in one view

One effect. Four explanations.
Two tests that separate them.

Confirmation · 512 routes

The story. Imagine a taxi driver recalling the last few turns to work out which intersection they are at. Call two parts of that memory A and B. Disturb both, and the driver loses their bearings. Which part explains the trouble? Four simple stories could fit that first observation. We tested what each story predicts when only one part is disturbed.

Scope, decision criteria & sources

What was measured

GPT-2 XL TaxiGPT, 48 blocks. Current-node decoding at layer 18, at the final move of complete 27–34-move routes, all at their goal. 512 routes sampled without replacement from 76,135 eligible held-out routes. The five conditions share the same routes.

What “excluded” means

Each rule must match both split conditions within 10% casewise disagreement. An exclusion requires a simultaneous lower bound above 10% for at least one mismatch. Eight exact finite-population intervals share a 5% error budget. Baseline, full-effect, random-control and technical gates passed.

What remains open

The four rules describe intervention responses, not exclusive semantic roles for K and V. Context-dependent and graded accounts remain possible. This does not identify a complete localization algorithm or establish general navigation ability or methodological superiority. The local centroid fit prevents an exact numerical replication claim.
