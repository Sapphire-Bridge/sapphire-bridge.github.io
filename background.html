Felix Borck

About · Frankfurt am Main, Germany

Profile

A German philosopher focused on knowability, provability, and representational systems, currently using LLMs and transformer architectures to study these questions empirically.

Since 2018 I have worked on knowledge, logic, and language. In 2024 I started building AI-enabled systems; since late 2025 I have been running empirical research on how internal states in LLMs causally govern meaning-like behavior and how robust those explanations are under intervention.

I spent six years on my master's thesis, five of them on and around an argument against Williamson's epistemicist theory of vagueness. The coursework took one year while the rest was the argument. I considered the argument worth the delay, and that conviction is what eventually drove me into mechanistic interpretability, where the question, whether internal representations are monosemantic or polysemantic, turns out to be the empirical version of the same question about vague predicates.

Interests

  • What are the properties of representational systems? → language, mathematics, transformer representations

Contrarian Beliefs
  • Wittgenstein's theory of language is one cohesive story.
  • Williamson's theory of vagueness is systematically insufficient.
  • Gödel-style incompleteness extends to any representational system rich enough to encode its own syntax, extending to LLMs.
