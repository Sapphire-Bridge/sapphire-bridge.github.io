Approach · Long version

From formal theories of meaning to causal tests inside language models.

I use formal semantics to specify the target independently of the intervention result, then test how language models realize it internally.

The specification problem

A well-controlled causal intervention can show that manipulating an internal variable changes a measured outcome. It does not by itself establish that the experimental contrast validly operationalizes the claimed construct, nor does causal efficacy alone identify the computation behind the behavior. My work addresses this specification problem. Formal semantics does not guarantee construct validity. Its role is to make the relevant validity claim testable independently of intervention success by specifying target contrasts, invariances, and falsification conditions. I turn those specifications into controlled same-value/different-standard pairs with explicit falsification controls, then use causal mechanistic methods to investigate how models internally realize them.

Borderline cases

Supervaluation: A case is borderline when its degree lies within the band of admissible thresholds. Different admissible values of θ can therefore yield different classifications while the degree remains fixed. Tolerance concerns stability under small changes in degree; it does not require a label flip.
