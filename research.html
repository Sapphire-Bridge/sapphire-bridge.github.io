Research

Four papers around meaning and mechanistic interpretability, plus one shipped system.

A short index of what I've built. The arc moves from a philosophical seed about context-dependent meaning to causal tests on transformer LMs, and to a current limitation result on what SAE reconstruction fidelity actually buys.

01 Philosophical foundation

Context-Dependent Semantics in Large Language Models

Conceptual paper - philosophical framing.

Argues that decoder-only Transformer self-attention operationalizes a relational, context-dependent theory of meaning closer to Braun than to Kaplan. The empirical hook is that GPT-style embeddings exhibit low self-similarity and high anisotropy in deeper layers - the same token's representation shifts substantially with its context.

This frames the move from a philosophical claim about meaning to causal tests inside transformer models, which is what the next three papers carry out.

GPT-2 / GPT-3 embeddings: context dependence increases with depth (Ethayarajh-style self-similarity / anisotropy).
PDF in proceedings (p. 181)
02 AoM - Paper 2

Appearance of Meaning (AoM)

Mathematics determines token, not meaning.
Under review at JoLLI (Journal of Logic, Language and Information).

Identifies internal states in transformer LMs that causally control meaning-like preference margins under context-swap activation patching, validated with sham-controlled comparisons. Across GPT-2 and Qwen2.5 checkpoints, donor-directed effects are structured and depth-localized; matched sham interventions sit near zero.

Includes a fixed-depth target-specificity stress test (SDH) showing target-span concentration over nearby matched controls in the canonical artifact for non-GPT-2 models, constraining token-privileged implementation glosses without claiming global token-atomic exclusivity.

Structured, depth-localized donor-directed effects with near-zero sham baselines across GPT-2 and Qwen2.5.

03 MoM - Paper 3

Mechanics of Meaning (MoM)

Mechanism determines computation, not basis.
Completed manuscript.

Tests whether already-localized contextual control is better recovered in the raw residual stream or in a sparse learned feature basis. Compares matched raw residual and SAE interventions at shared resid_post sites in Gemma 2 2B across disambiguation, counterfactual-preference, and discourse-coherence tasks.

The result is heterogeneous, not uniform: a bounded early sparse-precision advantage shows up on DISAMB, while matched-rank PCA and random orthogonal controls make generic compression or rank-reduction alone insufficient explanations for the early L4 advantage.

Bounded sparse-precision effect appears only on early disambiguation - layer- and task-dependent, not a universal sparse advantage.

04 Limitation - Paper 4 - CRR

Geometric Fidelity Is Not Behavioral Preservation

Reconstruction fidelity determines geometry, not causality.
Draft available on request.

Shows that high SAE reconstruction fidelity does not validate sparse feature-level causal explanations of behavior. On Gemma 3 4B, layer 4 and layer 8 reach near-identical reconstruction quality (cosine 0.997 / 0.998, relMSE 0.007 / 0.007) but recover the matched raw-writeback effect very differently.

A matched-rank PCA control at L4 essentially recovers the raw effect (PCA effect 0.829 vs raw 0.815) despite having no interpretability claim at all - decoupling behavioral preservation from sparse interpretability.

L4 SAE causal recovery 0.621 [0.263, 0.954] vs L8 SAE 0.974 [0.749, 1.320], with identical relMSE 0.007. PCA control at L4 ≈ raw.
