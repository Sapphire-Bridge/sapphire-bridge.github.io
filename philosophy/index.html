From formal theories of meaning to causal tests inside language models.

I use formal semantics to specify the target independently of the intervention result, then test how language models realize it internally.

A changed answer shows causal leverage, not yet construct validity or a full account of the computation. Causal interventions do not by themselves establish that the tested contrast captures the claimed concept. My experiments therefore begin with theory-derived contrasts and falsification conditions, then ask which internal variables realize them.

Read the approach in full

In Gemma-3-4B, rank-5 layer-20 bases fitted on one size operationalization transferred causally to a held-out one and back, recovering 86% and 59% of the full-rank writeback effect against a no-op sham of 0.000. The two families share a prompt template, and the two fitted bases are not the same subspace. Public research note and aggregate results, not yet peer-reviewed. The two primary raw-data CSVs are not included in the public repository.

Same value, different standard. The same three-centimeter length counts as huge for an ant and tiny for a labrador.

I investigate how linguistic context shapes the evaluation of a fixed value. I explicitly specify comparison standards and test whether interventions on internal states can transfer the resulting shifts in size judgments. In separate behavioral tests, I also use standards that conflict with everyday expectations.

Matched control arms — directions for the value, the standard, their two-dimensional combination, norm-matched random directions, and a no-op sham — test whether the causal effect requires the standard-relative contrast rather than the absolute value.

For mechanistic interpretability: construct specifications and controlled contrasts that strengthen causal experiments.

For formal semantics and philosophy of language: a route from theories of meaning to falsifiable experiments inside models.

For empirical XAI and applied research: clearer evidence about what an intervention changes—and what that success does not yet establish.

Once an intervention produces the expected behavioral shift, I ask whether an interpretable representation preserves that intervention effect.

At layer 4, strong SAE state reconstruction did not certify preservation of the raw intervention effect.

In Gemma 3 4B, error-preserving SAE delta patching recovered an estimated 62.1% of the mean raw donor-state replacement effect at L4 (CRR 0.621; 95% CI 0.263–0.954), despite state-reconstruction cosine 0.997, relMSE 0.007 and FVU 0.137. At L8, CRR was 0.974 (95% CI 0.749–1.320), with cosine 0.998 and FVU 0.060. The L8 point estimate was close to raw; equivalence was not established. These percentages describe the measured intervention effect. Intervention definition, full five-layer profile and strict-gate sensitivity.
