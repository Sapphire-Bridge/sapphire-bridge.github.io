Geometric Fidelity Does Not Certify Behavioral Preservation

A short result page for the current matched-writeback finding: strong SAE reconstruction diagnostics can coexist with substantial under-recovery of a causally localized behavior.

Detailed technical draft available to researchers on request.
Main Claim

Reconstruction diagnostics alone do not certify sparse feature-level causal explanation. In Gemma 3 4B, layer 4 reaches reconstruction cosine 0.997, relMSE 0.007 and FVU 0.137, but sparse writeback recovers only CRR 0.621 [0.263, 0.954] of the matched raw writeback effect.

The point is not that sparse features fail in general. The narrower claim is that a sparse basis should be checked for behavioral preservation when it is used as an intervention substrate.

CRR is a recovery ratio relative to matched raw writeback, not a clipped probability; confidence intervals can therefore extend above 1. FVU is reported because cosine and relative MSE are both inflated by the residual stream's mean component: at layer 4 only about 5% of activation energy is variance about the layer mean. The pair-clustered FVU intervals overlap (L4 [0.126, 0.150], L8 [0.027, 0.216]), so the FVU point-estimate difference is not the inferential basis of the result.

L4 and L8 are the compact exposition pair, not the largest observed under-recovery gap: L11 has lower observed CRR than L4. The full five-layer profile is below.

Five-layer profile

The paper's declared inferential contrast is the paired SAE-minus-raw difference at the same layer-token site, not a comparison of marginal raw and SAE intervals. Across all five tested residual-stream layers, that contrast is negative everywhere and excludes zero at three of five, while reconstruction cosine never falls below 0.997.

52 authored context pairs per layer, both donor directions, 520 comparability rows in total. The pattern is heterogeneous across depth rather than monotonic in reconstruction quality: L11 has the best cosine (0.9987) and the lowest recovery, and L5 reconstructs worse by FVU than L4 (0.180 versus 0.137) while recovering more (0.908 versus 0.621).

Control

A matched-rank PCA control sharpens the point. At layer 4, PCA writeback sits at 0.829, essentially at the raw level of 0.815, while SAE writeback drops to 0.506. At layer 8, the ordering reverses: SAE stays near raw at 0.907 versus 0.931, while PCA falls to 0.688.

The intended reading of this control is deliberately weak. PCA is fitted leave-one-pair-out on the evaluation distribution while the SAE is frozen and trained elsewhere, so PCA has an in-distribution advantage. It tests whether a rank-controlled low-rank projection preserves the effect, not whether sparsity is the pressure point, and it is not an interpretability substrate. At layer 4 the paired PCA-minus-raw contrast is +0.013 [−0.217, +0.247]: indistinguishable from raw, which is not the same as perfect.

Interpretation

The result asks a simple validation question. Once a behavior has been causally localized, does the proposed explanatory basis preserve that behavior when written back under the same intervention protocol?

For this controlled context-sensitive behavior, high reconstruction fidelity is compatible with under-recovery of the raw causal effect. Feature discovery and feature-level causal explanation should therefore be treated as separate achievements.

Caveats

The layer-4 interval excludes zero only narrowly, which warrants replication rather than generalization. The strict-gate sensitivity analysis makes that caution sharper: five of 520 rows exceed pre-specified invariance diagnostics and remain in the reported aggregates under the governed policy. Removing them leaves the L4 SAE-minus-raw estimate negative (−0.213, 95% CI [−0.465, 0.033]) but it no longer excludes zero by target sign-flip (p = 0.117, versus p = 0.008 under the governed policy). L8 stays closer to zero under the same gate (−0.055, p = 0.666). The direction is stable; the per-layer significance claim is inclusion-policy dependent.

Evidence is limited to one model family, one SAE width, and one behavior class. The SAE is a Gemma Scope width-16k JumpReLU trained for a different model revision than the one patched, so an SAE-provenance explanation of the layer-4 gap is not excluded by these runs.

This page is a short public-facing summary of the headline CRR result and the PCA control. It does not reproduce the full manuscript, stress arms, or cross-model analyses. Those materials will be released with the public version of the work.
