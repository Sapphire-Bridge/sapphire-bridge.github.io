Geometric Fidelity Is Not Behavioral Preservation

A short result page for the current matched-writeback finding: near-perfect SAE reconstruction can coexist with substantial under-recovery of a causally localized behavior.

Detailed technical draft available to researchers on request.
Main Claim

Reconstruction fidelity alone does not validate sparse feature-level causal explanation. In Gemma 3 4B, layer 4 reaches reconstruction cosine 0.997 and relMSE 0.007, but sparse writeback recovers only CRR 0.621 [0.263, 0.954] of the matched raw writeback effect.

The point is not that sparse features fail in general. The narrower claim is that a sparse basis should be checked for behavioral preservation when it is used as an intervention substrate.

Headline Numbers
Gemma 3 4B site Raw writeback SAE writeback Fidelity CRR [95% CI]
Layer 4 0.815 0.506 cos 0.997 · relMSE 0.007 0.621 [0.263, 0.954]
Layer 8 0.931 0.907 cos 0.998 · relMSE 0.007 0.974 [0.749, 1.320]

CRR is a recovery ratio relative to matched raw writeback, not a clipped probability; confidence intervals can therefore extend above 1.

Control

A matched-rank PCA control sharpens the point. At layer 4, PCA writeback sits at 0.829, essentially at the raw level of 0.815, while SAE writeback drops to 0.506. At layer 8, the ordering reverses: SAE stays near raw at 0.907 versus 0.931, while PCA falls to 0.688.

This supports a depth-heterogeneous interpretation: behavioral preservation depends on the site and basis, and standard activation-fidelity metrics do not predict the causal recovery pattern.

Interpretation

The result asks a simple validation question. Once a behavior has been causally localized, does the proposed explanatory basis preserve that behavior when written back under the same intervention protocol?

For this controlled context-sensitive behavior, high reconstruction fidelity is compatible with under-recovery of the raw causal effect. Feature discovery and feature-level causal explanation should therefore be treated as separate achievements.

Caveats

This page is a short public-facing summary of the headline CRR result and the PCA control. It does not reproduce the full manuscript, broader layer sweeps, stress arms, or cross-model analyses. Those materials will be released with the public version of the work.

Code and release artifacts are prepared for public release. Until then, implementation details are available only through the controlled manuscript/release package.
